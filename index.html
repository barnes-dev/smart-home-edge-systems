01 / Why this exists

The mission

“The future of the smart home belongs at the edge, not in the cloud. The future of helpful AI should live there too.”

Our mission is to dismantle reliance on subscription-heavy, high-maintenance cloud servers by testing, benchmarking, and engineering simple, cost-effective, and entirely private local ecosystems for every home. That includes practical AI: Ollama models running inside the lab to support local voice assistants, vision, coding, automation, and private document search. We measure the real tradeoffs between model capability, response latency, memory, power, and hardware cost so families can choose systems that fit their needs instead of accepting opaque cloud subscriptions as the only path to a smart home. We want to provide a low-cost, one-stop, tried-and-tested solution for integrating secure infrastructure for this Smart Home Edge System.

Our voice testing promise

We will test the complete local path, not just the model in isolation: from the satellite microphone, across the private backend, through Home Assistant and Ollama, and back to the speaker. Every result should be explainable, repeatable, and useful to the community.

01 / CaptureMicrophone and wake wordSignal integrity, gain, noise floor, and false triggers.
02 / UnderstandSTT and transcriptionRecognition accuracy and processing time for real commands.
03 / DecideIntent and local AIHome Assistant actions, Ollama response time, and model behavior.
04 / RespondPiper and TTSTime to first audio, format compatibility, and playback quality.
05 / ProveLogs and network evidencePipeline timings, packet paths, CPU/GPU use, and privacy checks.

A core privacy condition: when the satellite is idle, it should not stream household audio to the backend. Network captures and pipeline logs will help verify that behavior.

05 / The person behind the measurements

About the architect

EB

Ed Barnes

Director & Lead Tester / Smart Home Edge Systems - Home Lab

Ed Barnes is a retired veteran with a deep, multi-decade understanding of the technological evolution of voice assistant ecosystems, from early high-latency cloud architectures to modern local-first environments. Following a career cut short due to a disability, Ed has channeled his disciplined engineering mindset, rigorous testing methodologies, and dedicated full-time focus into pioneering open-source, private-by-default home automation.

Utilizing an enterprise-grade testing lab, a dedicated media production studio, and extensive hands-on experience deploying containerized virtualization architectures, Ed is building a definitive open benchmark data matrix for the next generation of local voice infrastructure. His goal is to provide the global open-source community with transparent, empirical performance data that bridges the gap between hardware manufacturers and private smart home adopters.
